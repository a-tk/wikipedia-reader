# Privacy Policy for Audio for Wikipedia

Last updated: 2026-11-03

Audio for Wikipedia reads Wikipedia articles aloud. It does not collect, sell,
or share personal data, and it has no analytics, advertising, or tracking.

## What data the extension handles

- **Your settings.** Your chosen voice, speaking speed, pitch, volume, and
  which kinds of content to skip (tables, infoboxes, and so on). These are
  stored with your browser's extension storage. If you are signed in to Chrome
  with sync turned on, Chrome syncs them across your devices as part of your
  browser profile. We never receive them.
- **Article text.** To read an article aloud, the extension reads the text of
  the Wikipedia page you are viewing, inside your browser. This text is not
  stored, logged, or sent to us or to any server run by us.

## Network use

The extension makes no network requests of its own and has no server.

## Text-to-speech voices

Speech is produced by your browser's built-in speech synthesis. Some voices are
installed on your computer and work entirely on-device. Other voices — for
example the "Google …" voices in Chrome — are provided by an online service, and
your browser sends the text being read to that service to turn it into audio.
This is done by the browser, not by this extension, and is governed by that
provider's privacy policy. Choose a voice supplied by your operating system if
you want the text to stay on your device.

## Third parties

The extension uses no third-party services, libraries, or SDKs at runtime, and
no data is shared with third parties.

## Retention and deletion

Settings stay until you change them or remove the extension. Removing the
extension deletes its stored settings from your browser.

## Changes to this policy

If the extension's data practices change, this policy will be updated and the
date above revised before the change ships.
